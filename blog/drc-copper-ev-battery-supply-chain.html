DRC Copper Output and the EV Battery Supply Chain: Who Controls the Transition?

The DRC's Structural Position in the Battery Metals Market

The Democratic Republic of Congo's Copperbelt — straddling the provinces of Haut-Katanga and Lualaba — is one of the most significant mineral concentrations on Earth. In 2025, the country produced approximately 3.1 million metric tonnes of copper and accounted for roughly 73% of global cobalt output. Both metals sit at the centre of the lithium-ion battery supply chain that underpins the global energy transition.

That structural position confers leverage — but converting geological advantage into durable economic benefit requires navigating a set of upstream, midstream, and downstream dynamics that have, to date, largely favoured Chinese processing and refining interests.

Offtake Architecture: Who Holds the Terms

The dominant model in DRC copper and cobalt offtake is the prepayment/streaming arrangement, under which Chinese smelters and trading companies — Glencore excepted — have provided capital to mining operations in exchange for long-term offtake at prices set against LME benchmarks with fixed discounts. This model transferred execution risk to the buyer while locking producers into below-spot pricing during the early years of EV demand growth.

The Cobalt Institute estimates that by 2025, approximately 68% of DRC cobalt production was committed under existing offtake arrangements, the majority flowing to Chinese cathode precursor (pCAM) manufacturers. The implication for spot-market traders is a structurally thin free float — price discovery increasingly happens at the margin.

Copper: LME Grade A vs. DRC Cathode Reality

DRC exports are predominantly copper cathode Grade A, meeting LME deliverable specification (99.99% purity). Key export corridors are:

  • Kolwezi – Kasumbalesa – Lusaka – Dar es Salaam: The dominant road/rail corridor for cathode destined for Asian markets
  • Kolwezi – Lobito (Angola) Atlantic Corridor: The Lobito Corridor, now benefiting from a USD 553 million rehabilitation programme, offers a shorter maritime route to European buyers and is increasingly competitive
  • Kolwezi – Beira (Mozambique): Used selectively for Southern African offtakers

Transit times on the Dar es Salaam corridor average 18–22 days by road-rail combination. Insurance and security costs on the Kasumbalesa crossing have been material operational considerations; border congestion remains a bottleneck for high-volume export programmes.

The Value Addition Imperative

The DRC government's 2022 mining code amendments and the subsequent push for in-country beneficiation have generated significant industry debate. The government has signalled an intent to restrict exports of unprocessed cobalt concentrate, requiring producers to smelt domestically. In practice, the smelting capacity in-country remains limited — Chemaf and a handful of Chinese-backed facilities notwithstanding.

For traders structuring copper cathode mandates out of the DRC, the regulatory environment in 2026 is stable for cathode (already a finished product) but carries some uncertainty for mixed hydroxide precipitate (MHP) and cobalt hydroxide exports as policy positions on beneficiation evolve.

Demand Outlook and Price Sensitivity

The consensus view from Wood Mackenzie and CRU (as of late 2025) projects a structural copper deficit emerging between 2027 and 2030 as EV penetration in China, the EU, and the US accelerates and new mine supply fails to keep pace. DRC production growth is constrained by power infrastructure (the Inga III hydroelectric project remains in development), skilled labour availability, and the capital intensity of new shaft development.

For Wambugu Kamotho clients considering copper mandate arrangements, the medium-term price signal is constructive. The operational priorities are corridor selection, clear title verification, and counterparty due diligence on the Congolese side — all areas where experienced in-country representation is non-negotiable.
